I am **Tadanaga Takahashi**, a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at the **University of New Mexico**. My academic interests lie in teaching mathematics, numerical analysis, and scientific computing. I earned my Ph.D. in Mathematical Sciences from the **New Jersey Institute of Technology** in 2023, where I also completed my bachelor's degree in 2017.

## Teaching

Since spring 2024, I have taught **Calculus III**, **Applied Ordinary Differential Equations**, and **Introduction to Numerical Computing** at UNM. Before joining UNM, I taught Calculus I as an adjunct instructor at NJIT and worked as a teaching assistant for courses including precalculus and calculus.

I approach teaching by developing mathematical ideas as a story: what motivates a method, how it works, and where its limitations lie. I design examples that emphasize the main concepts, and I use visualizations and computational experiments to help students connect theory with practice.

You can find course outlines and selected teaching materials on my [Teaching page]({{ '/teaching/' | relative_url }}).

## Research

My research focuses on **numerical methods for partial differential equations**, especially domain decomposition methods. Much of my work concerns finite element methods for Helmholtz wave-scattering problems, including transmission conditions and the treatment of cross-points where subdomains meet. My doctoral research also addressed domain decomposition methods for the nonlinear Monge–Ampère equation.

For more information, see my [Research page]({{ '/portfolio/' | relative_url }}) and [Publications]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}).
