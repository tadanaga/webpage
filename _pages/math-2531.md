<h2 id="syllabus">Course syllabus</h2>

### 1. Course information

**Course:** MATH 2531 — Calculus III  
**Institution:** University of New Mexico  
**Credits:** 4  
**Instruction:** Lectures and recitation; term-specific arrangements are posted on Canvas.

The course develops three-dimensional geometry, differentiation of vector-valued and multivariable functions, multiple integration, and introductory vector calculus.

### 2. Learning objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Work with vectors and describe lines, planes, and common surfaces in three dimensions.
- Differentiate and integrate vector-valued functions and interpret velocity, acceleration, and curvature.
- Compute partial and directional derivatives, gradients, and tangent planes.
- Find and classify extrema, including constrained extrema using Lagrange multipliers.
- Set up and evaluate double and triple integrals in appropriate coordinate systems.
- Evaluate line integrals and apply the fundamental theorem for line integrals and Green's theorem.

### 3. Course materials and prerequisites

**Prerequisite:** MATH 1522 (Calculus II) or its equivalent.

**Textbook:** James Stewart, *Calculus*, 9th edition (used in a previous coordinated offering; the assigned edition may vary).

**Software:** No programming software is required for the general outline. Graphing and visualization tools such as Desmos may be used for demonstrations.

**Course resources:** Lecture notes, assignments, announcements, and grades are distributed through UNM Canvas for each semester.

### 4. Assessment and grading

The following is an **illustrative grading breakdown from a previous coordinated offering**, not a fixed policy for every semester.

| Assessment | Weight |
|:--|--:|
| Weekly homework | 15% |
| Quizzes | 10% |
| Three midterm exams | 50% |
| Cumulative final exam | 25% |

**Homework:** Regular problem sets emphasize complete, clearly explained solutions. Written submissions are typically collected through Canvas as PDFs.

**Exams:** Timed midterm exams assess computational skill, conceptual understanding, and mathematical reasoning. The final exam is cumulative. Exact exam rules and arrangements are given in the current semester syllabus.
