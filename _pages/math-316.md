**University of New Mexico**

An introduction to ordinary differential equations, their solution methods, and applications in the physical and natural sciences.

## Curriculum

The course is organized around the topics below. The order and emphasis may vary slightly from semester to semester.

### 1. First-order differential equations

- Introduction to differential equations, initial-value problems, and verifying solutions
- Direction fields and solution curves
- Separation of variables
- Linear first-order equations and integrating factors
- Applications of first-order equations
- Autonomous equations: equilibrium solutions, phase lines, and stability
- Population models
- Existence and uniqueness of solutions

### 2. Second-order linear equations: homogeneous problems

- Spring–mass models: free vibration with and without damping
- Characteristic equations and distinct real roots
- Repeated characteristic roots
- Complex characteristic roots
- Interpreting solutions of unforced spring–mass systems

### 3. Second-order linear equations: nonhomogeneous problems

- Method of undetermined coefficients
- Forced spring–mass systems
- Beats and resonance

### 4. Laplace transforms

- Definition, linearity, and transforms of basic functions
- Solving initial-value problems with transform tables and partial fractions
- Shift formulas
- Step functions and piecewise-defined functions
- Piecewise forcing and convolution
- Impulsive forces

### 5. Linear systems of differential equations

- Linear algebra review: matrices, determinants, inverses, and solving linear systems
- Vector-valued functions, their derivatives, and trajectories
- Systems of the form \(\mathbf{x}' = A\mathbf{x}\)
- Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
- Phase portraits for distinct real eigenvalues: saddles and nodes
- Zero, complex, and repeated eigenvalues
- Spirals, centers, star nodes, and degenerate nodes

### 6. Nonlinear systems and applications

- Rewriting second-order equations as first-order systems
- Equilibria and linearization
- Competing-species models
- Lotka–Volterra predator–prey model
- Nonlinear mechanics and the pendulum
