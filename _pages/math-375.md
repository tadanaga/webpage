<h2 id="syllabus">Course syllabus</h2>

### 1. Course information

**Course:** MATH/CS 375 — Introduction to Numerical Computing  
**Institution:** University of New Mexico  
**Credits:** 3  
**Instruction:** Mathematical analysis and MATLAB-based numerical experiments.

The course explores numerical algorithms for differentiation, root finding, linear systems, interpolation, integration, eigenvalue problems, approximation, and differential equations, with particular attention to convergence, stability, and computational cost.

### 2. Learning objectives

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Explain truncation error, rounding error, conditioning, and convergence of numerical approximations.
- Implement numerical algorithms in MATLAB and evaluate their accuracy and computational cost.
- Solve linear systems using direct and iterative techniques, and understand when each is appropriate.
- Apply root-finding methods to nonlinear equations and compare convergence behavior.
- Construct interpolating polynomials and splines and assess approximation errors.
- Approximate derivatives and integrals using numerical formulas.
- Use numerical techniques for eigenvalue problems, least-squares approximation, and initial-value problems.

### 3. Course materials and prerequisites

**Prerequisites:** MATH 2531 (Calculus III) and CS 261 (Mathematical Foundations of Computer Science), as listed in the Spring 2026 course syllabus. Background in programming and differential equations is helpful.

**Textbook:** Ward Cheney and David Kincaid, *Numerical Mathematics and Computing*, 7th edition (6th edition also suitable for the referenced offering).

**Software:** MATLAB is used for algorithm implementation, experiments, and written computational assignments.

**Course resources:** Slide PDFs, assignments, announcements, and grades are provided through UNM Canvas.

### 4. Assessment and grading

The following **illustrates the grading breakdown used in Spring 2026**. The number and weight of midterm exams may vary between semesters.

| Assessment | Weight |
|:--|--:|
| Homework | 30% |
| Midterm exam 1 | 20% |
| Midterm exam 2 | 20% |
| Cumulative final exam | 30% |

**Homework:** Assignments combine mathematical derivations, numerical calculations, and MATLAB programming. Written reports, including appropriately labeled figures and code, are normally submitted as PDFs through Canvas.

**Exams:** Written exams assess numerical methods, mathematical reasoning, and the interpretation of numerical results. The final exam is cumulative; semester-specific exam rules are provided separately.
