My lectures follow the development of an idea: what motivates it,
how it works, and where it falls short. Concrete examples,
guided questions, and computational demonstrations help
students connect mathematical methods with the reasoning
behind them.

## University of New Mexico

Since spring 2024, my teaching as a Postdoctoral Fellow has
included the following courses:

- [**MATH 2531 — Calculus III**]({{ '/teaching/math-2531/' | relative_url }})
- [**MATH 316 — Applied Ordinary Differential Equations**]({{ '/teaching/math-316/' | relative_url }})
- [**MATH/CS 375 — Introduction to Numerical Computing**]({{ '/teaching/math-375/' | relative_url }})

Course materials include typed lecture notes and organized Canvas
modules, with Desmos for visual exploration and MATLAB for
computational experiments.

For historical context on the people behind these methods, see my
[Mathematicians Through Time]({{ '/timeline/' | relative_url }}) page.
